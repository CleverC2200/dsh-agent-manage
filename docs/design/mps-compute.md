# MPS calculation contract

Manage owns the browser-safe pure function `computeMpsWorkflow` in the public `dsh-agent-manage/mps-compute/workflow` module. The package exposes source, JavaScript and declaration paths through `./mps-compute/*`. Workbench normalizes the current evaluation draft, source read status, relation snapshot and same-version shipment result. This module performs no reads, writes or authorization.

The input contains an evaluation context, product dimensions, explicit category demand, natural-month sales submissions, previous-complete-fiscal-year shipments, opening inventory, source capacity candidates and local constraints. Nonempty categories from the current product catalog establish the category scope. Each remains present for all four months when its demand is missing. Demand for a category outside that catalog is retained in `excludedCategoryDemand` with its original values and reason; it cannot expand scope or accept an adjustment. Empty product catalogs create no category rows. The former fixed `mpsCategories` list is removed. M+1 equals the evaluation month. M+4 remains at category level. Rows at base, material group and SKU grain exist for M+1 through M+3.

The current product-detail module defines the material-code pool, category and material-group codes. Source names and series do not establish identity. Workbench supplies business sales-plan base/SKU `allnum` quantities from `agents_scm_dimension_plan_rate_mi` and complete annual shipment quantities from `agents_scm_cust_deliv_daily_di`. The fiscal year runs July through June; `previousFiscalYear` selects the preceding complete fiscal year for each evaluation month. The calculator's `report` and `history` row months both identify the target evaluation month; Workbench retains the historical source start/end dates separately. The evaluation month fixes the submission period, product/base domain and preceding complete fiscal year for one set of split coefficients. M+1, M+2 and M+3 multiply their own effective category demand by that set. Later-month submissions do not change this evaluation's coefficients, including across July. Monthly quantity or share adjustments remain scoped to their edited month.

After a complete successful submission read, Workbench supplies zero for absent in-scope base/SKU records and retains that policy in the demand export. Existing NULL values, conflicting keys and incomplete or failed reads remain unknown. When every base/SKU submission quantity in a valid category is zero, its demand uses historical shares at 100% instead of averaging with submission shares. `splitBasis` records this choice. Missing historical quantities still block affected results.

For each month and category, each source independently exposes material-group/category, in-scope SKU/group and base-SKU/in-scope-SKU quantities and ratios. Denominators use that source's returned records matching the current product-detail keys. A missing row stays null and does not add a zero or invalidate the other source's existing records. A returned null quantity, duplicate product key, failed current read or zero denominator prevents the dependent share. Out-of-pool records are retained in `excludedSources` and do not enter these denominators. A current-product quantity without a base remains a blocker unless its quantity is explicitly zero. Its affected category comes from the current product catalog, including historical-code mappings; a different source classification does not turn that category-specific gap into a blocker for every category. Results remain in-scope until nationwide coverage is established. The largest material group receives one minus the sum of the other group shares. Direct base-SKU/category shares from both sources are averaged before multiplying by category demand. Zero intermediate denominators remain null in the layer formulas; a complete positive category can still establish a true zero direct share. No piece-rounding rule is assumed; the last positive-weight row receives only the floating-point residual needed to reconcile with category demand.

Category demand uses only the current MPS product-table SKU pool and its categories. Workbench reclassifies recent BI actuals, allnum plans, historical monthly shipments and annual SKU plans through this pool. Out-of-pool or ambiguously classified records remain in a separate completion list and do not block uniquely classified pool records. Nationwide category totals retain valid source quantities without requiring a base; base-level workflow rows still require their base keys. Category demand remains the explicit input seam of the workflow. Each input row carries effective `quantity` and may supply `systemQuantity` separately; an absent baseline uses `quantity` for existing callers, while explicit null preserves an unknown baseline. Effective adjustments apply to their own demand month and downstream SKU demand and inventory, preserving original system values. Workbench now supplies calculated values through `computeMpsCategoryDemand` from `mps-compute/category-demand`. Opening plans use the three months ending two months before the evaluation month; adjusted plans end one month before it. The statistical base M is the month before the evaluation month, using the same-version effective shipment forecast from Workbench; the recent actual window remains the attainment and annual-estimate basis. Each historical year aggregates nationwide category quantities before calculating factors; year-on-year factors use the equal-weight mean of valid annual factors within the most recent three years and return their numerator months in `yoyYears`. Each historical year also returns `numeratorMonth`, `denominatorMonth`, the two quantities, `ratio`, `valid` and `excludedReasons`; `yoyValidCount` and `yoyComplete` distinguish partial coverage from all three usable groups. Missing, invalid or zero-denominator annual factors are skipped; a true zero numerator remains valid, and no valid years leaves the result null. Month-over-month and annual monthly-share factors still require three complete years. M+1 and M+2 successively multiply the statistical-base shipment forecast and preceding month-over-month result by their historical monthly ratios. M+3 and M+4 continue that independent chain using adjacent annual-plan month ratios; weighted demand and manual demand adjustments never enter this chain. All four months take the mean of month-over-month and annual-estimate share quantities. Only M+1 then averages that mean with sales submission multiplied by aggregate BI actual/allnum plan attainment. M+2, M+3 and M+4 use the two-method mean directly and do not depend on sales submissions or plan attainment. The result is expected shipments; no subsequent division by attainment applies. Sales submissions follow the selected opening or adjusted quantity, independently of historical allnum plans. `shipment` and `submissions` supply already-scoped category/month quantities; missing or duplicate category-month records remain unknown. Results expose `methodAverage`, `salesSubmission`, `salesCorrected` and `weighted` for the calculation preview and export. All four months expose their historical monthly share, share-method quantity and two-method mean. M+2 through M+4 retain null for sales submission, sales correction and final sales weighting. Historical source reads cover the recent-window months and all four demand months across calendar-year boundaries. The attainment denominator supplied through checks.plan is the same-window allnum plan quantity from ai_db.agents_scm_dws_scm_cust_plan_exe_di. Workbench owns its verified schema, aggregation and source coverage. Opening/adjusted mode changes the three-month window. Incomplete plan coverage blocks final correction while independent BI methods remain available. No model prediction substitutes for these inputs. Missing category demand does not remove valid shares, opening inventory or source capacities. Negative projected closing inventory is preserved as opening inventory. A source income field missing from historical shipments does not invalidate valid shipment quantities.

Workbench may provide evaluation-period `reportRatios` at group, SKU and base grain from the existing dimension-plan source. Ratios recomputed from current product-group quantities take precedence. Workbench accepts a source SKU/group ratio only when its source group matches the current product group by code or name; old source-group ratios cannot override a regrouped quantity. When submitted quantities are missing, the largest historical material group and each group's historical Top1 SKU receive one minus the known sibling ratios. Equal historical quantities are ordered by their group/code keys. Explicit duplicate, invalid or negative-residual ratios remain unavailable; missing non-Top1 siblings and base shares are not invented. Raw quantities remain null, while `coefficientFallback` records each applied residual. Fully known split ratios must reconcile before the complete category can be allocated. Each material group's fallback SKU for final-share adjustments is previous-complete-fiscal-year in-scope Top1, with equal quantities ordered by code. Unadjusted inputs retain the direct two-source shares. Accepted final-share edits leave other nonfallback shares unchanged; the fallback absorbs the group residual and distributes it in proportion to its original averaged two-source base-SKU/category shares. Explicitly edited fallback base rows remain fixed while the remaining fallback bases split the residual using their normalized original system shares. If every fallback base is edited, the group must balance exactly. Invalid, duplicate, incomplete-source or negative-residual edits are rejected through `adjustmentIssues`, preserving the group's system shares. Category and final-share edits are optional public inputs. Results retain system and effective values; effective category edits apply to their own month's SKU demand and downstream inventory dependencies. `adjustments.quantities` accepts M+1/M+2 quantity edits at material-group, SKU or base-SKU grain through `MpsQuantityAdjustment`. All edits carry month, category, group and quantity; `kind: 'sku'` also carries code, and `kind: 'base-sku'` carries code and base. At each level an edited child stays fixed, while the remaining quantity is distributed among unedited children using their effective split weights. The last positive-weight child absorbs floating-point residuals; zero weights stay zero. Invalid scope, duplicate edits, unknown weights or fixed children exceeding their parent reject that category/month's quantity edits. A category/month with final-share edits rejects quantity edits so one effective quantity remains authoritative. Result rows retain `systemDemand`, effective `demand` and `quantityAdjusted`; effective shares are derived from accepted quantities. Removing an edit recomputes from the current inputs. The caller owns reasons, timestamps, edit history, identity and draft persistence.

Safety inventory uses effective target turnover days multiplied by next month's effective demand divided by that next calendar month's days. Zero safety days establishes zero safety inventory even when next demand is unknown; absent safety days remains unknown. The third SKU month uses fourth-month category demand and third-month effective shares only as an inventory reference. No fourth-month SKU row is created. Required production is `max(0, demand + targetInventory - openingInventory)`. Opening inventory accepts signed same-version previous-step quantities and then rolls forward through unconstrained projected closing inventory. `evaluation: 'unconstrained'` applies to all rows, including later months after an overload. `mainPlan` remains null because these calculations do not create an executable schedule.

Capacity requires explicit positive SKU tons-per-piece coefficients, unique base resource IDs, tunnel/forming resource kinds, base-month production days and explicit SKU resource allocation shares summing to one within each kind. Allocation candidates and daily ton/piece ratios never establish a weight or an allocation. Legacy capacity candidates remain separate display facts, and SKU monthly capacity, load and gap fields remain null. Resource loads use allocated required tons divided by daily tons and production days. Loads below 90% are normal, 90% through 105% are warning, and above 105% are overload. Resource-level excess days and ton gaps use the same allocations. Explicit zero capacity/days leaves load undefined while preserving computable ton gaps; production days cannot exceed calendar days. Missing either resource kind, invalid allocation or weight prevents a complete evaluation. Shared resources appear once per month. Unknown allocations also keep unassigned candidate resources unknown.

Each row and resource returns nullable quantities and missing reasons. The summary separates the strict total, known ton subtotal and evaluated scope; incomplete scope does not imply full feasibility. Local inputs do not represent formal saving or an active scheduling rule. Outsourcing and merged-base mappings still require explicit business decisions. The constrained solve described below consumes explicit transfer eligibility and produces a separate schedule.

`tests/mps-workflow.test.mjs` exercises the public pure function with worked inputs, zero values, missing data, source coverage and reconciliation. `pnpm test:mps-compute` runs these checks. The normal TypeScript build emits the JavaScript and declaration files; Workbench's author build resolves the public source export and bundles it for the browser.

## Executable monthly constraints

`constraints.rules.local` accepts version-local structured rules with a stable `id`, description, source, hard/soft strength, optional base/SKU/category scope, inclusive effective months and a discriminated `effect`. Supported effects are inventory-day floors, production-day caps, uniquely identified resource shutdowns, self-production requirements, transfer-ton ceilings, subcontract cost-increase ceilings and minimum new-product lead days. Text-only legacy records remain drafts until their effect is specified. Disabled, duplicate or malformed rules do not silently execute. A soft rule changes calculation only after explicit adoption.

Inventory floors feed target inventory, required production and subsequent inventory carry. Production-day caps limit known calendars and never fill an unknown actual calendar. Resource shutdowns set the matching resource's capacity to zero only in applicable months and retain the resulting real gap. Independent resources and demand quantities remain intact.

Monthly `observations` contain a value and its source for the rule's complete target scope. Transfer, cost and lead-time rules compare this evidence against their threshold; missing, duplicate or unsourced evidence remains pending. A self-production restriction reports a conflict when a proposed outsourced quantity is positive or known self-production capacity is insufficient. These checks do not create a transfer, subcontract or production schedule.

The result exposes `ruleEvaluations` with rule ID, month, status, affected SKU count, observed value, limit, unit and reason. Applicable hard conflicts and missing evidence prevent affected rows from being counted as fully evaluated; source quantities and known partial results remain available. Workbench owns edits, adoption/relaxation decisions and their subject/Session-scoped change history. No rule or relaxation writes to production systems.

### Target turnover days and missing-value supplements

Monthly source records are keyed by base, SKU, month and inventory version in `ai_db.agents_scm_goods_inventory_turnover_data_m`. Effective days use `final_target_turnover_days`, independently of source inventory and daily demand. Their quotient remains a separate diagnostic. Repeated same-key values must be complete and equal; conflicting final turnover days are retained as conflicts rather than selecting an arbitrary row. Zero days is valid.

Automatic comparison version selection chooses one version for the whole source month, preferring correction, initial, then rolling. Missing SKU records or empty fields do not fall back to another version. Turnover-day selection fixes one source month (`inventoryTurnoverMonth`, defaulting to the evaluation month) and one version for all three evaluation months; explicit version selection requires that version. Workbench passes the actual read start month, keeping it separate from the evaluation month. New evaluation months default to the current month through day 20 and the next month afterward, including year transitions; an explicitly selected historical month is preserved. Current and past source months prefer correction then initial; future source months prefer initial then rolling. An explicit day conflict remains a blocker rather than silently choosing a lower version. After selection, the same base/SKU in the current product category and material group uses one turnover-day value across M+1 through M+3. Only the selected source month supplies days to all three evaluation months. Other source months cannot fill missing days or create a conflict. Conflicting selected source keys require manual completion. Missing source records never use the diagnostic quotient or an automatic 25-day value.

`constraints.turnoverDays` contains manual base/SKU supplements for missing or conflicting source days. Existing explicitly maintained `constraints.safetyDays` base values remain a fallback when no SKU supplement exists. Valid source days take priority over both supplements; adopted inventory-day rules apply afterward. Workbench lists missing and conflicting base/SKU rows in a separate editable table and persists supplements by evaluation version. Source facts are reread for the active identity. `turnoverPolicy` exposes the common source value, source months and missing reasons. Source inventory diagnostics preserve their monthly key, selected version, status and missing codes separately from effective target-inventory dependencies. Each row exposes `nextMonth`, the month whose effective demand is used for target inventory. A valid source day value does not imply this demand is available; zero effective days still require no next-month demand.

## Capacity-constrained three-month solve

The public `mps-compute/plan` module exports `solveMpsPlan`. It reuses the demand/share and target-inventory calculation, then schedules the first three months in order. `computeMpsWorkflow` remains the unconstrained reference calculation. Workbench executes the constrained solve once in Tab6, stores an identity- and version-scoped result in memory, and displays that same result in Tab7. Changed drafts, source snapshots or identities invalidate export until reevaluation. A page reload requires another solve; formal report saving is not connected.

Physical capacity is tunnel daily tons multiplied by valid base-month production days after adopted day caps and shutdowns. Forming-machine input is outside this solve. Every output allocation consumes a unique physical resource and is checked against its capacity, including production for other bases. Workbench groups source tunnels by base, workshop and tunnel number; repeated equal capacities are deduplicated, conflicting capacities are excluded. Explicit local tunnel resources replace source resources for their base. Source SKU-line relations are eligible alternatives, rather than fixed production shares. Manual eligibility retains its configured month. Missing calendars, weights, demand, opening stock or targets remain unknown, while independent complete SKUs can be scheduled.

A confirmed zero category demand produces zero SKU demand without requiring split shares; missing share evidence remains available for manual completion. The same rule applies to a zero M+4 category used as next-month inventory demand. Unknown category demand remains unknown. Zero demand still requires opening inventory and a valid inventory target because an inventory shortage can require production. A zero net production requirement needs neither a weight nor a production resource. Workbench exports demand, opening-inventory and target-inventory exceptions with their source fields and manual completion actions; these exceptions do not prevent independent complete rows from continuing.

Within a production stage, the `fair-allocation` module uses a bipartite flow to maximize the common fulfillment ratio of net production requirements. For requirements of 80 and 20 tons sharing 50 tons of capacity, it supplies 40 and 10 tons. Alternative resources can be reassigned. A bottleneck freezes the affected SKUs at their common ratio; remaining SKUs continue with available resources. Original demand is unchanged. Quantities remain continuous because no batch-size or whole-piece rounding rule has been agreed.

Local demand is allocated first. Explicit source-to-destination paths then permit remaining demand to use a producing base's remaining capacity. Workbench selects the latest stat_period snapshot from the transfer-direction source and uses that snapshot for all three production months, retaining its source period independently of demand months. Complete configured paths may pass through intermediate bases. Path traversal ignores cycles and respects any explicit month restriction supplied by other callers. Every base on an internal-production path requires a unique, matching SKU weight. Production consumes capacity only at the producing base; each configured leg records equal outgoing and incoming quantities, and intermediate bases retain balanced transfers without invented production or inventory. Enforced transfer ceilings trim matching transfer-leg tons. Costs and lead-time rules with missing or conflicting evidence block affected rows. Internal production allocations and ODM default arrivals remain separate. No automatic commercial disposition is generated.

Projected closing inventory equals opening stock plus physical production plus ODM default arrivals plus imports minus exports minus fulfilled demand. The next month carries this allocated closing stock, even when the inventory target was not reached. ODM arrivals are estimates, so this is not evidence of actual received stock. Unfulfilled production requirements and fulfilled sales demand remain separate fields; unmet demand does not silently change the next month's original demand. Known production at a base with incomplete inventory inputs remains a partial production subtotal, not a complete inventory balance. When an allocation produces or passes through a base/SKU outside the demand domain, the plan includes a partial row for that month with its known production, transfer-in and transfer-out quantities. Demand, fulfillment and closing stock remain unknown, and the row does not expand the coefficient domain or invent production in later months. Source coverage gaps remain visible and do not imply nationwide completeness.

At execution, Workbench may reuse complete, ready source relations, including the latest transfer-direction snapshot selected during that read, only for the same draft, evaluation month, identity/Session scope and query configuration, with a recorded configuration version. Busy or failed reads, explicit refresh, changed scope or configuration, and identity changes or cancellation invalidate reuse. Otherwise Workbench rereads the relations and selects the latest transfer-direction snapshot. Paired counts and details select the same maximum source period; mixed source periods in a collection are rejected. Coefficients use the existing channel-aware weight source; conflicting channel values are not averaged. Workbench supplies a calendar with 25 baseline production days per month, subtracting the five natural dates before and five after Spring Festival in the months where those dates fall. The festival date itself is not subtracted again. An explicit production-day override is the final value; unavailable festival dates in January or February require manual completion. The unconfirmed 0.5 transfer/inventory correction is not applied. The report distinguishes pre-solve demand load, which may exceed capacity, from actual resource usage, which must never exceed capacity.

### ODM external default arrivals

ODM is external supply only. `odmProductCodes` identifies the SKU domain, `odmSupplies` names the receiving base and logical supplier, and `odmConflicts` preserves contradictory source evidence. Workbench obtains ODM evidence from explicit ODM capacity classification or the latest transfer snapshot's ODM label. A receiving base must be explicitly labeled ODM. ODM tunnel placeholders never become internal resources. A conflicting self-production label, internal line assignment or mixed receiving label requires manual correction; neither source silently overrides the other. The current source lacks actual supplier names, so `odm:default` is an estimated grouping, not a verified supplier.

For a single receiving base, ODM默认计划到货量 is `max(0, effective demand + target closing inventory - available opening inventory)`, in pieces per month. Demand100, target20 and opening30 produce arrival90, internal production0 and projected closing20. Internal planned production is never invented or subtracted. Multiple demand bases contribute their individual net shortages to one supplier/SKU/month schedule. Each allocation records external arrival once at its receiving base and equal transfers on each configured downstream leg. Intermediate bases do not manufacture. Out-of-domain receiving or transit rows preserve unknown demand, inventory and fulfillment, alongside known arrival and transfer quantities.

ODM never consumes internal production capacity, and missing internal daily capacity alone does not block it. Piece plans can proceed without weight when no enforced transfer-ton constraint depends on it. Ton reporting stays unknown without a unique consistent coefficient; an enforced ton ceiling still requires it. Self-only rules prohibit external supply; cost and lead rules require valid evidence at every applicable node. Transfer ceilings apply jointly to internal and external transfer legs. Missing demand, opening inventory or target inventory remains unknown. Known zero requirements produce zero arrivals. Closing stock carries month by month using the same estimate and allocated quantities.

The UI and exports name the field ODM默认计划到货量 and state that it is a default estimate, not verified supplier capacity, delivery timing or actual receipt. Internal load reports exclude ODM demand and show it separately. Source conflicts, missing receiving routes and other blocking inputs include source fields and manual completion instructions in the classified issue CSV.
