# MPS calculation contract

Manage owns the browser-safe pure function `computeMpsWorkflow` in the public `dsh-agent-manage/mps-compute/workflow` module. The package exposes source, JavaScript and declaration paths through `./mps-compute/*`. Workbench normalizes the current evaluation draft, source read status, relation snapshot and same-version shipment result. This module performs no reads, writes or authorization.

The input contains an evaluation context, product dimensions, explicit category demand, natural-month sales submissions, previous-complete-fiscal-year shipments, opening inventory, source capacity candidates and local constraints. Nonempty categories from the current product catalog establish the category scope. Each remains present for all four months when its demand is missing. Demand for a category outside that catalog is retained in `excludedCategoryDemand` with its original values and reason; it cannot expand scope or accept an adjustment. Empty product catalogs create no category rows. The former fixed `mpsCategories` list is removed. M+1 equals the evaluation month. M+4 remains at category level. Rows at base, material group and SKU grain exist for M+1 through M+3.

The current product-detail module defines the material-code pool, category and material-group codes. Source names and series do not establish identity. Workbench supplies business sales-plan base/SKU `allnum` quantities from `agents_scm_dimension_plan_rate_mi` and complete annual shipment quantities from `agents_scm_cust_deliv_daily_di`. The fiscal year runs July through June; `previousFiscalYear` selects the preceding complete fiscal year for each evaluation month. The calculator's `report` and `history` row months both identify the target evaluation month; Workbench retains the historical source start/end dates separately. The evaluation month fixes the submission period, product/base domain and preceding complete fiscal year for one set of split coefficients. M+1, M+2 and M+3 multiply their own effective category demand by that set. Later-month submissions do not change this evaluation's coefficients, including across July. Monthly quantity or share adjustments remain scoped to their edited month.

For each month and category, each source independently exposes material-group/category, in-scope SKU/group and base-SKU/in-scope-SKU quantities and ratios. Denominators use that source's returned records matching the current product-detail keys. A missing row stays null and does not add a zero or invalidate the other source's existing records. A returned null quantity, duplicate product key, failed current read or zero denominator prevents the dependent share. Out-of-pool records are retained in `excludedSources` and do not enter these denominators. A current-product quantity without a base remains a blocker unless its quantity is explicitly zero. Results remain in-scope until nationwide coverage is established. The largest material group receives one minus the sum of the other group shares. Direct base-SKU/category shares from both sources are averaged before multiplying by category demand. Zero intermediate denominators remain null in the layer formulas; a complete positive category can still establish a true zero direct share. No piece-rounding rule is assumed; the last positive-weight row receives only the floating-point residual needed to reconcile with category demand.

Category demand uses only the current MPS product-table SKU pool and its categories. Workbench reclassifies recent BI actuals, allnum plans, historical monthly shipments and annual SKU plans through this pool. Out-of-pool or ambiguously classified records remain in a separate completion list and do not block uniquely classified pool records. Nationwide category totals retain valid source quantities without requiring a base; base-level workflow rows still require their base keys. Category demand remains the explicit input seam of the workflow. Each input row carries effective `quantity` and may supply `systemQuantity` separately; an absent baseline uses `quantity` for existing callers, while explicit null preserves an unknown baseline. Inherited effective changes from earlier months continue into demand and inventory without replacing the original system values. Workbench now supplies calculated values through `computeMpsCategoryDemand` from `mps-compute/category-demand`. Opening plans use the three months ending two months before the evaluation month; adjusted plans end one month before it. The base is the final actual month. Each historical year aggregates nationwide category quantities before calculating factors; year-on-year factors use the equal-weight mean of valid annual factors within the most recent three years and return their numerator months in `yoyYears`. Each historical year also returns `numeratorMonth`, `denominatorMonth`, the two quantities, `ratio`, `valid` and `excludedReasons`; `yoyValidCount` and `yoyComplete` distinguish partial coverage from all three usable groups. Missing, invalid or zero-denominator annual factors are skipped; a true zero numerator remains valid, and no valid years leaves the result null. Month-over-month and annual monthly-share factors still require three complete years. The first two months average the chained month-over-month method and the annual-estimate share method, then divide by aggregate BI actual/allnum plan attainment. M+3 and M+4 extend the preceding result by adjacent annual-plan month ratios. The attainment denominator supplied through checks.plan is the same-window allnum plan quantity from ai_db.agents_scm_dws_scm_cust_plan_exe_di. Workbench owns its verified schema, aggregation and source coverage. Opening/adjusted mode changes the three-month window. Incomplete plan coverage blocks final correction while independent BI methods remain available. No model prediction substitutes for these inputs. Missing category demand does not remove valid shares, opening inventory or source capacities. Negative projected closing inventory is preserved as opening inventory. A source income field missing from historical shipments does not invalidate valid shipment quantities.

Workbench may provide evaluation-period `reportRatios` at group, SKU and base grain from the existing dimension-plan source. Ratios recomputed from current product-group quantities take precedence. Workbench accepts a source SKU/group ratio only when its source group matches the current product group by code or name; old source-group ratios cannot override a regrouped quantity. When submitted quantities are missing, the largest historical material group and each group's historical Top1 SKU receive one minus the known sibling ratios. Equal historical quantities are ordered by their group/code keys. Explicit duplicate, invalid or negative-residual ratios remain unavailable; missing non-Top1 siblings and base shares are not invented. Raw quantities remain null, while `coefficientFallback` records each applied residual. Fully known split ratios must reconcile before the complete category can be allocated. Each material group's fallback SKU for final-share adjustments is previous-complete-fiscal-year in-scope Top1, with equal quantities ordered by code. Unadjusted inputs retain the direct two-source shares. Accepted final-share edits leave other nonfallback shares unchanged; the fallback absorbs the group residual and distributes it in proportion to its original averaged two-source base-SKU/category shares. Explicitly edited fallback base rows remain fixed while the remaining fallback bases split the residual using their normalized original system shares. If every fallback base is edited, the group must balance exactly. Invalid, duplicate, incomplete-source or negative-residual edits are rejected through `adjustmentIssues`, preserving the group's system shares. Category and final-share edits are optional public inputs. Results retain system and effective values; effective category edits also feed subsequent annual-ratio months. `adjustments.quantities` accepts M+1/M+2 quantity edits at material-group, SKU or base-SKU grain through `MpsQuantityAdjustment`. All edits carry month, category, group and quantity; `kind: 'sku'` also carries code, and `kind: 'base-sku'` carries code and base. At each level an edited child stays fixed, while the remaining quantity is distributed among unedited children using their effective split weights. The last positive-weight child absorbs floating-point residuals; zero weights stay zero. Invalid scope, duplicate edits, unknown weights or fixed children exceeding their parent reject that category/month's quantity edits. A category/month with final-share edits rejects quantity edits so one effective quantity remains authoritative. Result rows retain `systemDemand`, effective `demand` and `quantityAdjusted`; effective shares are derived from accepted quantities. Removing an edit recomputes from the current inputs. The caller owns reasons, timestamps, edit history, identity and draft persistence.

Safety inventory uses base safety days multiplied by next month's effective demand divided by that next calendar month's days. Zero safety days establishes zero safety inventory even when next demand is unknown; absent safety days remains unknown. The third SKU month uses fourth-month category demand and third-month effective shares only as an inventory reference. No fourth-month SKU row is created. Required production is `max(0, demand + targetInventory - openingInventory)`. Opening inventory accepts signed same-version previous-step quantities and then rolls forward through unconstrained projected closing inventory. `evaluation: 'unconstrained'` applies to all rows, including later months after an overload. `mainPlan` remains null because these calculations do not create an executable schedule.

Capacity requires explicit positive SKU tons-per-piece coefficients, unique base resource IDs, tunnel/forming resource kinds, base-month production days and explicit SKU resource allocation shares summing to one within each kind. Allocation candidates and daily ton/piece ratios never establish a weight or an allocation. Legacy capacity candidates remain separate display facts, and SKU monthly capacity, load and gap fields remain null. Resource loads use allocated required tons divided by daily tons and production days. Loads below 90% are normal, 90% through 105% are warning, and above 105% are overload. Resource-level excess days and ton gaps use the same allocations. Explicit zero capacity/days leaves load undefined while preserving computable ton gaps; production days cannot exceed calendar days. Missing either resource kind, invalid allocation or weight prevents a complete evaluation. Shared resources appear once per month. Unknown allocations also keep unassigned candidate resources unknown.

Each row and resource returns nullable quantities and missing reasons. The summary separates the strict total, known ton subtotal and evaluated scope; incomplete scope does not imply full feasibility. Local inputs do not represent formal saving or an active scheduling rule. Outsourcing and merged-base mappings still require explicit business decisions. The constrained solve described below consumes explicit transfer eligibility and produces a separate schedule.

`tests/mps-workflow.test.mjs` exercises the public pure function with worked inputs, zero values, missing data, source coverage and reconciliation. `pnpm test:mps-compute` runs these checks. The normal TypeScript build emits the JavaScript and declaration files; Workbench's author build resolves the public source export and bundles it for the browser.

## Executable monthly constraints

`constraints.rules.local` accepts version-local structured rules with a stable `id`, description, source, hard/soft strength, optional base/SKU/category scope, inclusive effective months and a discriminated `effect`. Supported effects are inventory-day floors, production-day caps, uniquely identified resource shutdowns, self-production requirements, transfer-ton ceilings, subcontract cost-increase ceilings and minimum new-product lead days. Text-only legacy records remain drafts until their effect is specified. Disabled, duplicate or malformed rules do not silently execute. A soft rule changes calculation only after explicit adoption.

Inventory floors feed target inventory, required production and subsequent inventory carry. Production-day caps limit known calendars and never fill an unknown actual calendar. Resource shutdowns set the matching resource's capacity to zero only in applicable months and retain the resulting real gap. Independent resources and demand quantities remain intact.

Monthly `observations` contain a value and its source for the rule's complete target scope. Transfer, cost and lead-time rules compare this evidence against their threshold; missing, duplicate or unsourced evidence remains pending. A self-production restriction reports a conflict when a proposed outsourced quantity is positive or known self-production capacity is insufficient. These checks do not create a transfer, subcontract or production schedule.

The result exposes `ruleEvaluations` with rule ID, month, status, affected SKU count, observed value, limit, unit and reason. Applicable hard conflicts and missing evidence prevent affected rows from being counted as fully evaluated; source quantities and known partial results remain available. Workbench owns edits, adoption/relaxation decisions and their subject/Session-scoped change history. No rule or relaxation writes to production systems.

### Base safety-days policy and monthly inventory comparison

MPS accepts read-only monthly inventory targets keyed by base, SKU, month and inventory version from `ai_db.agents_scm_goods_inventory_turnover_data_m`. Source target days are `final_target_inventory / avg_daily_planned_demand`; a missing or nonpositive denominator, invalid target or conflicting key leaves the source days unknown. Repeated source rows are reduced to one key only when each value is complete and agrees; values are never summed across duplicate records. A real zero target with positive demand produces zero days. Targets do not expand the demand SKU domain. Each result returns a source `status` and `missingCodes` alongside the expected month/base/SKU key and selected version. An absent exact key stays missing; source normalization owns evidence about mismatched source keys. `missing-selected-version` distinguishes a key present only in another version. Source normalization may supply `conflictFields` for distinct-value conflicts in finalTarget or dailyDemand. The calculation reports `conflict`, clears only the conflicting field and prevents source days; a NULL alone remains invalid rather than proving a conflict. Known source target quantities remain visible when source days or the next-month derivation are unavailable. Workflow `targetInventoryMissing` separately identifies missing safety days, missing next-month effective demand and invalid derived quantities. Source read failures use `read-failed` and retain the caller's source messages.

Automatic version selection chooses correction, initial, then rolling once per base and month. Explicit selection requires that exact version; missing SKUs never borrow another version or period. Source days are SKU turnover diagnostics and do not supply the base safety-days policy. Each evaluation version maintains one nonnegative safety-days value per base in `constraints.safetyDays`, reused across all three result months. Missing, invalid or duplicate base strategies remain unknown; zero is explicit. Adopted inventory minimum rules still apply to their scope. Each row exposes the base strategy, effective days, next-month demand and next calendar month's natural days. Later months can calculate target inventory without monthly source inventory records when their policy and next-month demand are known. Source quantities are not persisted as local inputs; only the comparison version preference and base policies survive draft recovery, and business facts are reread for the current identity and evaluation.

## Capacity-constrained three-month solve

The public `mps-compute/plan` module exports `solveMpsPlan`. It reuses the demand/share and target-inventory calculation, then schedules the first three months in order. `computeMpsWorkflow` remains the unconstrained reference calculation. Workbench executes the constrained solve once in Tab6, stores an identity- and version-scoped result in memory, and displays that same result in Tab7. Changed drafts, source snapshots or identities invalidate export until reevaluation. A page reload requires another solve; formal report saving is not connected.

Physical capacity is tunnel daily tons multiplied by valid base-month production days after adopted day caps and shutdowns. Forming-machine input is outside this solve. Every output allocation consumes a unique physical resource and is checked against its capacity, including production for other bases. Workbench groups source tunnels by base, workshop and tunnel number; repeated equal capacities are deduplicated, conflicting capacities are excluded. Explicit local tunnel resources replace source resources for their base. Source SKU-line relations are eligible alternatives, rather than fixed production shares. Manual eligibility retains its configured month. Missing calendars, weights, demand, opening stock or targets remain unknown, while independent complete SKUs can be scheduled.

Within a production stage, the `fair-allocation` module uses a bipartite flow to maximize the common fulfillment ratio of net production requirements. For requirements of 80 and 20 tons sharing 50 tons of capacity, it supplies 40 and 10 tons. Alternative resources can be reassigned. A bottleneck freezes the affected SKUs at their common ratio; remaining SKUs continue with available resources. Original demand is unchanged. Quantities remain continuous because no batch-size or whole-piece rounding rule has been agreed.

Local demand is allocated first. Explicit, unambiguous source-to-destination routes then permit remaining demand to use a producing base's remaining capacity. Routes stay within their source month and require a unique, matching SKU weight at both bases. Each transfer records equal outgoing and incoming quantities. Enforced transfer ceilings trim matching transfer tons. Costs and lead-time rules with missing or conflicting evidence block affected rows. No subcontract allocation or automatic commercial disposition is generated.

Actual closing inventory equals opening stock plus physical production plus imports minus exports minus fulfilled demand. The next month carries this actual closing stock, even when the inventory target was not reached. Unfulfilled production requirements and fulfilled sales demand remain separate fields; unmet demand does not silently change the next month's original demand. Known production at a base with incomplete inventory inputs remains a partial production subtotal, not a complete inventory balance. Source coverage gaps remain visible and do not imply nationwide completeness.

Workbench rereads source relations and each of the three months' transfer directions at execution. Coefficients use the existing channel-aware weight source; conflicting channel values are not averaged. The current calendar baseline is 25 production days for ordinary months and 15 for February 2027. Other February calendars require explicit maintenance. The unconfirmed 0.5 transfer/inventory correction is not applied. The report distinguishes pre-solve demand load, which may exceed capacity, from actual resource usage, which must never exceed capacity.
